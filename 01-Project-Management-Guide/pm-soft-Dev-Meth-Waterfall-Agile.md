## 🔄 **Software Development Methodology**

A **software development methodology** is a structured approach or framework that guides how software projects are planned, executed, and delivered. It defines the **processes, practices, and rules** teams follow to ensure software is built efficiently, meets user needs, and aligns with business goals.

Different methodologies exist to suit different project contexts — some emphasize **predictability and documentation** (like Waterfall), while others focus on **flexibility and collaboration** (like Agile and Scrum).

### 🧱 **Waterfall Methodology**

-   A **linear, sequential model** where each phase (requirements → design → development → testing → deployment → maintenance) must be completed before moving to the next.
    
-   **Strengths:** Clear structure, strong documentation, predictable timelines.
    
-   **Limitations:** Inflexible to change, late feedback, risk of misalignment if requirements evolve.
    
-   **Best For:** Projects with **stable, well‑defined requirements** (e.g., compliance systems).
    

### 🔄 **Agile Methodology**

-   An **iterative, adaptive approach** that delivers work in short cycles (sprints). It emphasizes collaboration, customer feedback, and flexibility.
    
-   **Strengths:** Rapid delivery, continuous feedback, adaptability to change.
    
-   **Limitations:** Requires disciplined teams, less formal documentation, harder to scale in large organizations.
    
-   **Best For:** Projects with **dynamic requirements** (e.g., startups, evolving products).
    

### 🏃 **Scrum Framework**

-   A **specific Agile framework** that organizes work into sprints (2–4 weeks).
    
-   **Key Roles:** Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team.
    
-   **Artifacts:** Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment.
    
-   **Events:** Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective.
    

**Scrum and Agile project management** are modern approaches to delivering projects that emphasize flexibility, collaboration, and continuous improvement.

## 🔄 **Agile Project Management**

-   Agile is an **iterative and adaptive methodology** for managing projects. Instead of rigid, sequential phases (like Waterfall), Agile delivers work in **short cycles (sprints)** and adapts based on feedback.
    
-   **Core Principles (Agile Manifesto):**
    
    -   Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.
        
    -   Working software over comprehensive documentation.
        
    -   Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
        
    -   Responding to change over following a plan.
        
-   **Purpose:** Enables teams to respond quickly to changing requirements and deliver value continuously.
    

## 🏃 **Scrum Framework**

-   Scrum is a **specific Agile framework** that organizes work into **sprints (2–4 weeks)** with defined roles, events, and artifacts.
    
-   **Roles:**
    
    -   **Product Owner****:** Owns product vision and backlog.
        
    -   **Scrum Master****:** Facilitates process and removes blockers.
        
    -   **Development Team****:** Builds product increments.
        
-   **Events:**
    
    -   **Sprint Planning** → Define sprint work.
        
    -   **Daily Scrum** → 15‑minute team sync.
        
    -   **Sprint Review** → Inspect increment with stakeholders.
        
    -   **Sprint Retrospective** → Reflect and improve.
        
-   **Artifacts:**
    
    -   **Product Backlog** → Ordered list of all work.
        
    -   **Sprint Backlog** → Subset of backlog for sprint.
        
    -   **Increment** → Completed, potentially shippable product.
        


✨ **Insight** — Think of **Agile** as the **mindset** (values and principles) and **Scrum** as one of the most popular **frameworks** that puts Agile into practice. Agile says _“be flexible and deliver value iteratively”_, while Scrum gives you the **roles, events, and artifacts** to make that happen in real projects.

### 👥 **Scrum Team**

A **cross‑functional group** responsible for delivering increments of value. It includes:

-   Product Owner (defines priorities),
    
-   Scrum Master (facilitates process),
    
-   Developers (build the product).
    

### ⏱️ **Sprints**

Time‑boxed iterations (usually 2–4 weeks) where the team delivers a **potentially shippable product increment**.

### 📋 **Sprint Backlog**

A **subset of the product backlog** selected for a sprint, containing tasks and user stories the team commits to completing.

### 📦 **Backlog**

A **prioritized list of work items** (features, bug fixes, enhancements) maintained by the Product Owner. It evolves continuously based on feedback.

### 📝 **Task**

A **small, actionable unit of work** derived from a backlog item or user story. Tasks are assigned to team members and tracked during the sprint.

### 🔍 **Micro**

Refers to **small‑scale, detailed activities** or granular tasks within a project. Example: coding a login function.

### 🌍 **Macro**

Refers to **large‑scale, high‑level activities** or overall project goals. Example: delivering the entire HR management system.

✨ **Insight** —

-   **Waterfall** = rigid, sequential.
    
-   **Agile/Scrum** = flexible, iterative.
    
-   **Backlogs & sprints** = tools for prioritization and delivery.
    
-   **Micro vs Macro** = zooming in on tasks vs zooming out on overall goals.
